Photo-Matching: How a Match Worn Shirt Is Verified

November 2026 · 6 min read

Photo-matching is the most respected way to show that a shirt was worn in a particular match. The idea is simple: find photos or footage of the player in that match, and check that the shirt in your hands is the same physical shirt. The practice is harder than it sounds.

What is compared

A single detail proves very little. A convincing match uses several features that are hard to fake and unlikely to repeat on another shirt:

  • Printing: the exact position, spacing and shape of the name and number, including small flaws such as a lifted edge or an uneven curve.
  • Patches and sponsor prints: placement, any wrinkle or bubble in the material, and whether the competition badges match that fixture.
  • Wear and damage: pulls in the fabric, small tears, repairs, stains and marks.
  • Construction: collar, cuffs and seams, particularly any irregularity.
  • Season, kit and competition: the right shirt for that match, with the right patches for that stage of the competition.

Why it is not simple

Clubs often prepared several identical shirts for a single player. A shirt can match the photos perfectly and still be a spare that stayed in the bag. Photos can also be low resolution, taken from a distance or from the wrong side. That is why a strong match depends on small, individual marks and not on the design alone.

What a good match report looks like

A trustworthy listing shows the work. You should see the match, the date and the photos used, with the points of comparison marked. If the seller only says "photo-matched" and shows nothing, treat it as unproven.

Photo-matching and certificates

A certificate from a club or a recognised issuer adds weight, and photo-matching adds more. Neither is a guarantee on its own. Our guide to certificates of authenticity explains what a certificate can and cannot prove, and our guide to match worn, match issued and player issue explains the wording.

Where it cannot be established

Many good shirts have no photo evidence at all. That does not make them fakes, and it does not make them worthless. It means the honest label is match issued or player issue, and the price should say the same. Browse our match worn and issued shirts to see how we describe each one.
