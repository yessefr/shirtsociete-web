Buying Vintage Football Shirts in the Netherlands

July 2026 · 5 min read

The Netherlands is a good country to collect football shirts in. Dutch football has a rich shirt history of its own, the country sits within easy reach of the German fair circuit, and there is an active collecting community. But finding genuine originals still takes knowing where to look.

Buy from someone who handles the shirts

The single most important decision is who you buy from, because with vintage there is no manufacturer standing behind the product. There is only the seller and what they tell you.

A specialist who collects as well as sells has handled thousands of shirts and knows what a period-correct label, a genuine badge application and honest wear look like. That knowledge is what you are paying for, and it is why a described condition can be trusted rather than guessed at.

The alternative is buying blind from someone who does not know what they have. That occasionally produces a bargain and regularly produces disappointment, and with vintage there is rarely a way back once the shirt is in your hands.

Why buying within the Netherlands helps

Shipping is quick and cheap, and there are no customs charges or import VAT to worry about, which is a real consideration when buying from outside the EU. Returns are simpler if something does not fit. And you have the protection of Dutch and EU consumer law, including the fourteen day right of withdrawal when buying from a business.

That last point matters more than people realise with vintage. If a shirt arrives and the fit is wrong, you want that to be straightforward.

What to check before you buy

Ask for measurements rather than trusting the size label, because vintage sizing bears little relation to modern sizing. Ask for photos of the tags, the wash label and the badge stitching. Ask directly whether the shirt is an original from the period or a later reproduction, and expect a clear answer.

For anything sold as match worn, ask which fixture it relates to and what documentation comes with it. A seller who knows their material will answer without hesitation.

Dutch shirts worth looking out for

The Oranje kits of the late eighties are the obvious starting point, and they exist in several market variants that are interesting to collect side by side. Beyond the national team, Ajax, Feyenoord and PSV shirts from the nineties have a strong following, and shirts from smaller Eredivisie clubs are far rarer simply because fewer were made and fewer were kept.
